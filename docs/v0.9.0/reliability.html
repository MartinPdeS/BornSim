Reliability and statistical interpretation#

BornSim separates numerical stability, equation consistency, statistical uncertainty and physical validity. No single diagnostic certifies all four.

Numerical refinement#

ConvergenceReport(results=..., labels=..., tolerance=0.01) compares the highest order of each full angular result. It reports relative changes in mu_s and mu_a, absolute changes in g, and the worst final-order equation residual across realizations. report.rows returns fresh numeric records; report.summary() returns readable text. A row marked stable means only that the compared observables changed less than the chosen tolerance. Reports omit nonfinite g values, such as the zero-scattering limit.

When both results supply nonzero scattering and the full polar output range, the report also compares azimuth-averaged phase curves with an L1 solid-angle difference. It linearly interpolates their sampled curves onto the union of cos(theta) coordinates and integrates with trapezoidal weights. This diagnostic can detect changes missed by mu_s and g alone. It is omitted for partial polar outputs and does not test azimuthal structure or replace quadrature refinement.

Change one control at a time: grid spacing at fixed physical extent, angular quadrature, Born order, sample extent, or ensemble size. Random fields sampled on different grids with the same seed are not guaranteed to represent the same continuous realization. Use deterministic geometry for clean grid studies, or compare random-medium ensembles with their sampling uncertainties.

result.residual_norms evaluates

\[r_M = \frac{\|E_{inc}+K\chi E_{[M]}-E_{[M]}\|}{\|E_{inc}\|}.\]

The array has one entry per cumulative internal-field order, or shape (realizations, order) for ensembles. It uses the same discretized integral operator as the solver and includes both incident polarizations. Born-series recursion makes this equal to the norm of the next omitted field term divided by the incident-field norm. The final order requires one extra Green operation. A small residual establishes consistency with the discrete equation, not accuracy of the mesh or linearized constitutive model. Absorption reported at scattering order M still uses the internal field through M-1.

See Explicit refinement studies and equation residuals.

Absorption and uncertainty#

Material(refractive_index=..., extinction_coefficient=...) assigns passive loss to regions before voxelization. A background Material fills uncovered sample voxels with that absorption; the exterior propagation host stays real and lossless. Structures replace both real index and kappa according to the overlap policy. Additive fluctuations add their real-index and kappa fields. Gaussian, exponential and Whittle-Matern media accept a uniform nonnegative extinction_coefficient; their ensemble realizations retain it.

Ensembles report mu_a and mu_a_stderr (sample standard deviation divided by sqrt(N)). One realization has unknown absorption uncertainty, represented by NaN. These errors quantify sampling uncertainty, not systematic model error. Existing Volume.with_absorption remains available for arbitrary voxel maps.

See Absorbing samples and Absorbing material and an optical-theorem check.

Coherent and diffuse scattering#

At each order and observation direction, let f_j be the cumulative scattered amplitude of realization j. Ensemble results expose

\[D_{coh} = \frac{|\overline f|^2}{V},\qquad D_{diff} = \frac{\overline{|f|^2}-|\overline f|^2}{V}.\]

Here squared magnitudes include the average over incident polarizations and sum over outgoing Cartesian components. coherent_differential contains D_coh; diffuse_differential contains D_diff; differential is their sum. Coherent refers to the mean scattered field, excluding the direct beam. Azimuth averaging happens after this separation, not before amplitude averaging.

These are empirical population-normalized estimates: diffuse scattering has finite-N factor (N-1)/N bias, and coherent intensity contains a positive finite-sample variance contribution. With N=1, diffuse is zero by construction, not evidence of a physically coherent medium. They are not independently normalized phase functions or automatically bulk transport coefficients. The components, residuals and absorption statistics survive saving/loading and azimuth averaging. Old archives without them return None.

See Coherent and diffuse scattering in an absorbing ensemble.

Physical validation#

Independent automated references check weak-contrast lossless and absorbing spheres against Mie scattering and absorption cross sections, angular patterns, and the forward optical theorem. The Mie reference uses Riccati-Bessel series coefficients independently of BornSim’s Green operator. See the Mie coefficient equations. Tests use an equal-occupied-volume reference radius to separate staircase volume errors from propagation errors. Grid refinement and nominal geometry validation remain necessary. BornSim still omits the quadratic dielectric contrast at every order; these weak-contrast tests do not validate high-index particles or optically thick transport.
